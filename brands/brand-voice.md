# Brand Voice and Copy Style

## Global Non-Negotiables (All SBH Brands)
These apply across every brand, channel, and content type unless a brand-specific rule explicitly overrides.

- Australian spelling throughout (organise, colour, travelled, flavour)
- No em dashes (exception: Ardour — see below)
- No hyperbole
- No pretentious language
- No comparative or correlative structures: "Whether this or that," "From this to that," "It's not this, it's that," or similar
- No copy starting with directives (e.g. "Celebrate," "Experience," "Exchange")
- No prose ending with "Here..."
- No generic openers: "Here —" or "Nestled..."
- Tone: elegant and grounded, without correlative conjunctions or unnecessary prepositional phrases
- Factual USPs over vague emotional claims
- No overused words: scrumptious, delectable, indulge, elevated, "culinary journey," beauty (and a broader list of 20 maintained separately)
- SEO copy to be written line by line with non-negotiables applied continuously
- Meta Ads for cold audiences: elevated, evocative, factual — not vague

## Spicers Retreats

### Tone
Warm. Lighthearted. Knowledgeable. Relaxed luxury — the tone of a confident host who loves what they do and doesn't need to prove it.

### Energy
Inviting without being pushy. Enthusiastic but never breathless. Specific and grounded, not sweeping and vague.

### Voice Descriptors
Inviting, lighthearted, knowledgeable. Never formal. Never pretentious. Never boring.

### What It Sounds Like
- Real language from one person to another
- Subtle urgency, not desperate urgency
- Idioms over colloquialisms ("hats off to our chefs" not "onya boys")
- Contractions are encouraged (we're, can't, you're)
- Puns used sparingly and with taste
- Leadership language — industry leaders, not followers

### What It Does Not Sound Like
- Bragging ("Our food is amazing")
- Sarcasm or ridicule
- Overfamiliar ("sanga", "yummy")
- Platitudes ("where luxury meets nature")
- Overtelling

### Tone by Context
- **Social (organic):** Warm and conversational. Hook-led. Short sentences. Text on screen for Reels.
- **Paid ads (cold):** Evocative and factual. Lead with a specific USP or setting detail. No vague luxury language.
- **SEO landing pages:** Clear and informative. Written to match search intent. Non-negotiables applied line by line.
- **Email:** Friendly and personal. Offer-led but not discount-feeling. Subtle urgency.
- **FAQs (Sangoma):** Specific to babymoon packages. Grounded in actual guest questions.

### Grammar and Punctuation
- No capitals in headlines (exception: retreat names always capitalised)
- Brands, spirits, regional products capitalised (Wagyu Beef, Champagne)
- Abbreviations: no full stops or spaces between initials
- Ampersands only in titles or space-limited contexts
- Numbers: zero–nine written out, 10+ as numerals
- Times: 12-hour clock, lowercase, no space (9.00am, 5.00pm)
- Dates: 15 July 2018 (not 1st August or 01/08)
- Ellipsis: always three dots, no space before next word
- One exclamation mark maximum — never in conjunction with other punctuation
- Website: spicersretreats.com (no www, no full stop)
- Wi-Fi (not WIFI or wifi)

## Ardour Hotels & Estates

### Tone
The Ultimate Host. Confident, warm, easy-going. Speaks like someone you actually want to talk to. Never tries too hard. Gives guests permission rather than asking for it.

### Energy
Effortless charm. Sensory and specific. Social and celebratory. Sophisticated without being stiff.

### Voice Descriptors
Charismatic (effortless, charming, confident). Formal (relaxed, warm, human). Storytelling (specific, sensorial, evocative). Luxurious (elevated, refined, indulgent). Inviting (helpful, positive, active).

### What It Sounds Like
- First person to guests: "Welcome, James. I'd recommend a table for dinner — how about I book it for 7pm?"
- First person plural in general marketing: "Our rooms face the lake..."
- Sensory specificity: steam from the outdoor bath, weight of cotton sheets, first sip of a local wine
- Idioms for wit: "Lap up the luxury"
- Personality: "You didn't hear it from us, but..."
- Permission-giving: "Want to order dessert first?"

### What It Does Not Sound Like
- Third person ("Ardour loves wine pairings")
- Arrogant or comparative ("No other hotel compares to us")
- Platitudes ("Luxury, reimagined")
- Puns on the brand name
- Overly formal ("We are delighted to" / "Please be advised")
- Tired constructions ("Goodbye city, hello countryside")
- "Iconic" used to describe own product

### Tone by Context
- **Paid ads:** Confident and sensory. Specific property details. Permission-giving CTAs.
- **Email:** First person, warm, personal. One emoji permitted in subject line for open rate.
- **Social:** Visual-led. Copy supports the image. Short, punchy, personality-forward.
- **On-property/collateral:** Intimate and direct. Handwriting-style font for welcome cards.

### Grammar and Punctuation (Ardour-specific overrides)
- Em dashes (—) are used — this is a deliberate Ardour brand rule
- En dashes (–) are not used
- Australian spelling throughout
- Numbers written as digits (3 things to do while you stay)
- Spell out "and" not "&"
- Always use a full stop
- Dates: Friday, 1 August 2025
- Avoid exclamation marks
- No emojis (exception: one in email subject lines)
- Contractions encouraged
- Favour full stops over commas for sharpness ("Check in. With your senses.")

## Worlds Apart

### Tone
Editorial and aspirational. A trusted curator, not a sales platform. Warm enough to feel personal, confident enough to feel authoritative.

### Energy
Discovery-led. Inspires before it converts. Feels like a luxury travel magazine recommendation, not a booking engine.

### What It Sounds Like
- Evocative but brief
- Property and experience-forward
- "A world of rewards that stand apart"
- Cross-brand without feeling generic

### What It Does Not Sound Like
- Promotional or discount-driven
- Overly brand-specific (not too Spicers, not too Ardour)
- Transactional or functional

### Key Brand Notes
- Tagline: "A world of rewards that stand apart"
- Brand credential: "A Worlds Apart Experience" — applied to Ardour, Spicers, and future brand collateral
- Rewards program categories: Rewards, Wellness, Dining, Events
- Typography: Heldane Text Regular (headlines), Inter Semibold (subheadings, all caps), Inter Regular (body)
- Colours: Charcoal (#282828), Sand (#F0F0E5), Grey (#969687), White. Neutral and restrained — no bright accents.
- Logo variants: Primary, Wordmark, Brand Mark (W-A monogram), Extended, Condensed

## Bannisters Hotels

### Tone
Relaxed coastal energy. Food and beverage-forward. Unpretentious. Convivial. Driven by the pleasure of good food, good wine, and great company.

### Energy
Classic coastal — not over-polished, not generic luxury. Mollymook feels more refined; Port Stephens ("Barefoot Luxury") feels more casual, sandy, and playful.

### What It Sounds Like
- Warm and inviting, with a coastal ease
- F&B-led: specific dishes, produce, Rick Stein context where relevant
- Social and shareable
- Shorter sentences, lighter touch than Spicers

### What It Does Not Sound Like
- Corporate or stiff
- Over-polished generic luxury
- Retreat-focused (that's Spicers territory)

### Key Brand Notes
- Brand tagline: "Better at Bannisters" — used as an accent/script element on collateral
- Rick Stein at Bannisters is always referred to in full — never shortened to "Rick Stein" alone
- Port Stephens positioning is "Barefoot Luxury" — copy should reflect a more casual, sun-drenched energy
- Mollymook copy skews more polished and classic coastal
- Typography: Montserrat headers (all caps), Source Sans Pro body, Portrait Script accent (sentence case only, never all caps)
- Colours: Bannisters Navy (#0A5178), White, Black. Ocean Spray Blue (#89B7F3) used sparingly
- Logo: one logo per artwork only. Never rotated or diagonal

## Styles to Avoid Across All Brands

| Pattern | Example | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Correlative conjunctions | "Whether you're here for rest or adventure..." | Formulaic, lazy |
| Directive openers | "Discover your perfect escape" | Generic, overused |
| Nested prepositional phrases | "In the heart of the hinterland, nestled among..." | Clichéd |
| Hyperbole | "Unrivalled," "world-class," "second to none" | Unsubstantiated |
| Vague luxury | "An elevated experience awaits" | Meaningless |
| Overused words | Scrumptious, delectable, indulge, elevated, culinary journey, beauty | Brand-flagged |
| Ending on "Here..." | "Your next escape starts here..." | Weak, overused |

## Admired Creative References
- Jacquemus — creative originality and emotional impact over polished lifestyle imagery
- Strong hooks (text on screen) as a content principle for Reels
- Batching workflows as a production principle

## Format Preferences by Content Type
- **SEO copy:** Prose-led, no padding, line by line with non-negotiables applied
- **Ad headlines:** Short, factual, no filler
- **Ad descriptions:** CTA-focused, one clear message
- **Social captions:** Hook first, concise, no generic sign-offs
- **Email subject lines:** Direct, curiosity-led or offer-led; one emoji permitted for Ardour
- **Long-form/landing pages:** Structured prose, no bullet-heavy layouts unless functionally necessary
