# ICP and Audience

## Overarching Audience Profile
Across all SBH brands, the core customer shares a mindset that values quality. They actively seek it out and are happy to pay for it. They are not defined by a single demographic but by a common orientation — curious, experience-driven, and motivated by the quality of the moment rather than the status of the brand.

---

## Spicers Retreats

### Primary Personas

**'Julie' — The Rewarder**
- Age: 45+, affluent
- Frequent traveller (2+ domestic holidays per year), frequent diner (3+ per month)
- Values quality, happy to pay for it
- Down-to-earth, witty, independent, ballsy
- Active, loves the outdoors
- Mindset: "This is my time. The kids are older and doing well. Now it's time to reap the rewards of all that hard work in my 30s."

**'Sam' — The Earner**
- Age: 25–44, affluent
- Leisure travel interest, dines out frequently (2+ per month)
- Ambitious, sassy, down-to-earth, independent, socially conscious
- Outdoorsy and fitness conscious
- Mindset: "I'm working hard, building my career and it will be totally worth it. But it's important to take time for myself as well."

### Booking Occasions
- Special occasions: birthdays, anniversaries, proposals, weddings, honeymoons, babymoons, life events, personal achievements, reunions
- Just for fun: eating out, spa treatments, holidays, romantic getaways, girls/friends getaways, active getaways, ticketed events
- Work and business: team building, strategy sessions, meetings and events, incentives, executive retreats, corporate travel

### Audience Language
- Values "relaxed luxury" — not stuffy, not transactional
- Responds to nature, food, wine, and authentic connection
- Motivated by the feeling of being known and looked after
- Looks for value but does not respond to discount language
- Appreciates specificity over generalisation

### Objections / Hesitations
- Price sensitivity at point of conversion — overcome with value framing, not discounting
- Distance from major cities for some properties — address with the reward of the journey and the setting
- "Is it worth it?" — overcome with specificity, social proof, and emotive copy

## Ardour Hotels & Estates

### Primary Persona (inferred)
**The Celebrant**
- Age: 30–55, high disposable income
- Socially motivated — travels to share experiences, not to escape them
- Food, wine, and culture-led
- Appreciates heritage, craft, and the story behind a place
- Wants to feel like an insider, not a tourist
- Motivated by occasion: anniversaries, milestone birthdays, long weekends, incentive travel
- Mindset: "I want a place that feels alive — somewhere with character, great food, and people who know how to host."

### Audience Language
- Responds to warmth and confidence, not formality
- Drawn to sensory specificity ("steam from the outdoor bath", "first sip of a local wine")
- Permission-giving language resonates — "you're allowed to do nothing", "order dessert first"
- Turned off by generic luxury clichés, over-formal tone, or anything that feels corporate

### Booking Occasions
- Milestone celebrations, anniversaries, long weekends
- Corporate retreats and incentive travel
- Weddings and private events
- Spontaneous getaways from Sydney/Canberra (Milton Park proximity)

## Bannisters Hotels

### Primary Persona (inferred)
**The Coastal Escapee**
- Age: 28–55, medium-high disposable income
- Food and beverage motivated — dining is central to the stay decision
- Social and relaxed — travels with partners, friends, or small groups
- Values coastal beauty, informality, and a great restaurant
- Likely based in Sydney or surrounds
- Mindset: "I want great food, an ocean view, and somewhere I don't have to think too hard."

### Audience Nuance by Property
- **Mollymook (By the Sea + Pavilion):** Polished coastal feel. Rick Stein at Bannisters is a primary draw — food-motivated audience. Classic, aspirational.
- **Port Stephens:** More casual, playful energy. "Barefoot Luxury" positioning — sun-drenched, sandy, relaxed. Slightly younger or more family-oriented energy.

### Audience Language
- Casual luxury — not white-glove, but not budget
- F&B-led copy resonates: specific dishes, produce, Rick Stein context
- Ocean and lifestyle imagery over nature/wilderness
- Social and shareable — this audience is platform-aware

### Booking Occasions
- Weekend escapes, long weekends
- Birthdays, anniversaries
- Friends and group getaways
- Food-motivated day trips that extend to overnight

## Worlds Apart (Platform Audience)

### Overview
Worlds Apart serves all of the above audiences, functioning as the discovery, booking, and loyalty layer. Tagline: "A world of rewards that stand apart." The platform operates a rewards program across four categories: Rewards, Wellness, Dining, and Events.

### Behavioural Profile
- Actively comparing properties and brands
- Responds to curated recommendations and offers
- Motivated by editorial-quality content and clear value propositions
- May not have brand loyalty to Spicers, Ardour, or Bannisters specifically — they are buying into an experience category

### Performance Notes
Retargeting audiences consistently outperform broad/cold targeting on Worlds Apart campaigns. Warm audiences — people who have previously engaged with SBH brand content or visited the platform — convert at a significantly higher rate. Cold prospecting through Worlds Apart should be treated as top-of-funnel awareness, not conversion-focused. Budget allocation should weight toward retargeting where possible.

### Key Message Territory
- "A world of rewards that stand apart"
- Discovery and aspiration — inspire before converting
- Cross-brand offers and packages that reward direct booking
- Loyalty and rewards program as a reason to book direct

## Independent Retreat Hotels

### Primary Persona (inferred)
**The Character Seeker**
- Avoids cookie-cutter hotel brands
- Values individuality, provenance, and a sense of place
- Often more adventurous in destination choice
- Quality-motivated but not brand-motivated
- Likely overlaps with Spicers and Ardour audiences in demographics but is driven by curiosity over brand recognition

---

## Shared Audience Truths Across All Brands
- Quality-seeking, not bargain-hunting — never respond well to discount-led messaging
- Experience over amenity — they want to feel something, not just be comfortable
- Australian domestic travel is their primary context — they know the landscape and are choosy about it
- Direct booking is preferred when the experience feels worth it — the platform and brand together need to earn that trust
- Sustainability matters but should be shown, not lectured
