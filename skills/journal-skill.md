# Skill: Journal Content (Worlds Apart)

## Purpose
Writing editorial content for the worldsapart.club journal at `/journal/`. Pieces are published by Worlds Apart as the platform brand, even when the subject is a Spicers, Ardour, Bannisters, or Independent property. This skill governs voice, structure, and the rules for writing about sub-brand properties without sliding into their voice.

---

## Scope
This skill applies to **editorial pieces published in `/journal/`** only.

SEO-driven destination guides housed under topical parents (e.g. `/experiences/guides/`, `/dine/guides/`) are governed by `seo-content-skill.md` instead — different intent, different voice register, different structural conventions. If a piece is being written to rank, it's an SEO guide and uses that skill. If it's being written to be read, it's a journal piece and uses this one.

---

## Output Format

### Page Structure
1. **H1** — editorial headline, not a keyword string. A journal title, not a landing page title.
2. **Intro** — lead with the idea, not the brand. Give the reader a reason to keep reading by the second sentence.
3. **Body** — prose-led. Subheadings (H2) used when they break the piece into genuine sections, not as visual padding.
4. **Lists** — only when functionally appropriate (e.g. "five Hunter Valley wineries worth the drive"). Default to prose.
5. **CTA** — soft and editorial. "See availability at [property]" or "Explore [category]". Not "Book now and save."

### Meta
- **Meta title:** under 60 characters. Keyword-first where it can read naturally, but still feels like an editorial title.
- **Meta description:** under 155 characters. Benefit-led, soft CTA.

## Whose Voice This Is

Worlds Apart platform voice applies. Not Spicers. Not Ardour. Not Bannisters. Even when a piece is about a specific property, the publisher is Worlds Apart, and the voice stays platform-level.

A useful frame: a Worlds Apart journal piece is what a luxury travel editor would write about a property they like — not what the property would write about itself.

### Writing About Sub-Brand Properties

**Naming**
- Full property name on first mention: Spicers Tamarind Retreat, Ardour Milton Park Bowral, Bannisters by the Sea
- Rick Stein at Bannisters: always in full, never just "Rick Stein"
- Subsequent references: short forms acceptable where natural (Tamarind, Milton Park)

**Tone**
- A piece about Spicers should not adopt Spicers' tone
- A piece about Ardour should not adopt Ardour's tone — em dashes do not carry across from Ardour into a Worlds Apart journal piece
- Avoid sub-brand-specific phrases unless quoting or framing them deliberately: "Better at Bannisters", "Relaxed Luxury", "The Ultimate Host", "Barefoot Luxury"

**Factual fidelity**
- Property details, room counts, restaurant names, chef partnerships, and locations must be accurate
- Source property facts from the relevant brand file in the marketing brain or the Sanity entry

## Voice Rules

### What It Sounds Like
- A trusted curator, not a sales platform
- Editorial and aspirational — like a luxury travel magazine recommendation
- Warm enough to feel personal, confident enough to feel authoritative
- Discovery-led — inspires before it converts
- Property and experience-forward
- Evocative but brief
- Cross-brand without feeling generic

### What It Does Not Sound Like
- Promotional or discount-driven
- Booking-engine functional
- Overly brand-specific (not too Spicers, not too Ardour, not too Bannisters)
- Transactional or operational

### Non-Negotiables
- Australian spelling throughout
- No em dashes
- No hyperbole (unrivalled, world-class, second to none)
- No comparative or correlative structures: "Whether X or Y", "From X to Y", "It's not X, it's Y"
- No directive openers: Discover, Experience, Celebrate, Explore
- No generic openers: Nestled, Situated, Set amidst, "Here —"
- No prose ending with "Here..."
- No overused words: scrumptious, delectable, indulge, elevated, culinary journey, beauty
- Factual USPs over vague emotional claims
- One exclamation mark maximum per piece, ideally none

### Brand References
- Tagline: "A world of rewards that stand apart" — used selectively, not in every piece
- Rewards categories: Rewards, Wellness, Dining, Events
- Brand credential: "A Worlds Apart Experience" — can be referenced when crediting properties

## SEO Inside the Voice

SEO is in scope. The editorial voice leads. Where they conflict:
- Keyword stuffing fails the voice test
- An H2 must read as an editorial section header first, an SEO heading second
- Search intent informs structure; voice governs execution

For ranking-led content (destination guides, "best of" pages), use `seo-content-skill.md` and publish under the relevant topical parent, not in `/journal/`.

## Examples

### Good — Intro (piece on Hunter Valley long weekends, featuring Spicers Vineyards Estate and Spicers Guesthouse)
The Hunter Valley rewards a slow weekend. Three days is enough to taste your way through Pokolbin, drive the back roads to Lovedale, and still have time to do nothing on a verandah. Two Spicers properties — Vineyards Estate and Guesthouse — make a strong base for it.

### Bad — Intro (same piece)
Discover the breathtaking beauty of the Hunter Valley with Spicers Retreats. Nestled in Australia's premier wine region, Spicers Vineyards Estate offers an elevated escape — whether you're a wine connoisseur or simply looking to indulge in the finer things in life.

*Why it fails: Directive opener (Discover), generic opener (Nestled), hyperbole (breathtaking, premier, elevated), comparative structure (Whether X or Y), overused words (indulge, elevated). Reads like a property brochure, not an editorial piece.*

---

### Good — CTA close
Spicers Vineyards Estate and Spicers Guesthouse both have weekend availability through Autumn. See rates and rooms on Worlds Apart.

### Bad — CTA close
Don't miss out on the ultimate Hunter Valley escape! Book now and save with our exclusive Autumn offer — your perfect getaway is just a click away.

*Why it fails: Promotional tone, hyperbole (ultimate), "don't miss out" urgency, generic ("your perfect getaway"), ends on a cliché.*
