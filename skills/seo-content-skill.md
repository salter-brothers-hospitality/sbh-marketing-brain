# Skill: SEO Content

## Purpose
Writing SEO landing pages and on-page content for SBH properties. Primary application is Spicers Retreats, with increasing work across Ardour and the broader portfolio.

---

## Output Format
- Prose-led. No bullet-heavy layouts unless functionally necessary (e.g. amenities list, inclusions).
- Structured around search intent — each section answers a specific question or serves a specific keyword cluster.
- H1: primary keyword, natural and specific. Not a brand tagline.
- H2s: secondary keywords and supporting intent. Scannable but not generic.
- Body copy: written line by line. Non-negotiables applied at every line, not just at review.
- Meta title: under 60 characters. Keyword-first where possible.
- Meta description: under 155 characters. Benefit-led, includes a soft CTA.

---

## Process
1. Identify primary keyword and search intent before writing
2. Review Surfer SEO or Ahrefs data for supporting terms
3. Write line by line — do not draft the full page and review at the end
4. Apply non-negotiables at each line: Australian spelling, no directive openers, no hyperbole, no overused words
5. Read back each paragraph aloud — if it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it
6. Check meta title and description last

---

## Voice Rules
- Australian spelling throughout
- No em dashes
- No directive openers (Discover, Experience, Explore)
- No generic openers (Nestled..., Situated..., Located...)
- No hyperbole (unrivalled, world-class, second to none)
- No overused words (scrumptious, delectable, indulge, elevated, culinary journey, beauty)
- No comparative or correlative structures
- No prose ending with "Here..."
- Factual USPs over emotional generalisations
- Tone: warm, grounded, specific — not brochure-ish, not clinical

---

## Page Types and Intent

### Transient / Accommodation Pages
- Intent: someone searching for a stay at a specific property or in a specific region
- Lead with what makes the property distinct — setting, character, specific details
- Include room types, inclusions, and what guests actually experience
- Avoid generic "luxury retreat" framing — be specific to the property

### Package / Offer Pages
- Intent: someone searching for a specific package type (all-inclusive, babymoon, meetings, etc.)
- Lead with the package benefit, not the property name
- Be specific about inclusions — what's in the rate, what dates apply, what the experience actually is
- Include a clear CTA section

### Destination / Category Pages
- Intent: someone searching for a type of experience in a region (e.g. "Blue Mountains retreat", "Hunter Valley accommodation")
- Lead with the destination or experience type, not the brand
- Introduce the brand/property as the answer to the search intent
- Include relevant destination context (region, proximity, what it's known for)

### Meetings and Events Pages
- Intent: someone searching for a conference or event venue
- Lead with capacity, spaces, and what the venue can accommodate
- Include specific room names, capacities, and configurations
- Tone slightly more functional than transient pages — decision-makers need facts

## Examples

### Good — Opening line (Spicers Sangoma, Blue Mountains all-inclusive)
Set on Bowen Mountain in the Blue Mountains, Spicers Sangoma Retreat is a private all-inclusive escape for up to 22 guests. Every stay includes accommodation, all meals, wine, and a daily spa treatment — so the only decision you need to make is when to arrive.

### Bad — Opening line
Nestled in the breathtaking Blue Mountains, Spicers Sangoma Retreat offers an elevated all-inclusive experience like no other. Indulge in world-class dining, luxurious spa treatments, and the natural beauty of one of Australia's most iconic landscapes.

*Why it fails: "Nestled", hyperbole, "like no other", "indulge", "elevated", "iconic", "natural beauty" — multiple non-negotiable violations in two sentences.*
